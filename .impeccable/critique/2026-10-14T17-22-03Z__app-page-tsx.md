# MAX homepage critique

Target: app/page.tsx; live evidence: https://max-modelling-tutor.vercel.app/.

The homepage has a coherent identity worth preserving. Notebook paper, the MAX mascot, violet lettering, and a prominent yellow student entry suit the product. The main weaknesses are clarity before entry and compact language controls, rather than a need for a wholesale visual replacement.

## What works

- The visual language is specific to mathematical learning rather than an interchangeable template.
- Student entry is dominant while staff links remain grouped and discoverable.
- Hebrew and Arabic switching works on the deployment. Narrow layouts stack role cards; the desktop capture shows two columns.

## Priority issues

### [P1] Make student entry predictable

The homepage says class code and name. The deployed student form also asks for surname, phone number, and address-as selection. This mismatch is verified on the deployment and in source. The homepage also does not explain where the class code comes from.

Fix: agree the intended entry requirements, then preview them accurately in both languages and add a short teacher-provided-code hint. Explain identification details where requested. Suggested commands: impeccable clarify, impeccable onboard.

### [P2] Explain MAX's teaching role

The introductory sentence explains turning a real situation into a mathematical model, but does not tell students that MAX supports their reasoning through questions and hints. This can create the expectation that MAX supplies the solution.

Fix: add one concise explanation of questions, hints, and student-owned reasoning. A small concrete modelling example could clarify the abstract term without adding a tutorial. Suggested command: impeccable clarify.

### [P2] Enlarge the language controls

Live DOM measurement reports Hebrew and Arabic button heights of 32 CSS pixels. They are compact controls on a page aimed at students.

Fix: target at least 44 pixels of usable height, retaining visible selected and focus states. This is a usability recommendation, not a claim that 32px alone violates WCAG. Suggested commands: impeccable adapt, impeccable polish.

## Cognitive load and emotional journey

The homepage itself has low decision load: two language choices and three role routes. No decision group exceeds four choices. Friendly copy and mascot support an approachable first impression. The main handoff problem is unexpected personal-information requirements after an easy-start promise.

## Persona checks

- First-time student: can find student entry, but cannot predict all required information or understand MAX's exact role.
- Mobile student: the whole student card is a generous target, while language controls are only 32px tall.
- Arabic reader: content switches correctly, but the browser title remains Hebrew.

## Minor observations

The institution credit is 12px on the measured live page and merits a modest readability increase. Arabic content has an Arabic main language annotation, but the document title stays Hebrew. Adjacent mascot and heading both announce MAX; assess whether the mascot should be decorative in this context.

## Directions to consider

Preserve the notebook identity while making the opening and entry guidance more informative. Choose between a focused clarity/touch-target pass and a broader layout refresh before implementation. Do not change the student-identification contract solely to make homepage copy easier.
